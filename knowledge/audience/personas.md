# Aurie Audience Personas

Three primary personas guide content creation. Rotate across them for diverse representation.

---

## Persona 1 — Anxious Ava

**Age:** 24
**Location:** Urban (New York, London, Sydney)
**Occupation:** Junior marketing coordinator / early-career professional

### Background
Ava graduated two years ago and is navigating her first "real" job. She performs well at work but
carries a constant low-level anxiety she can't quite name — is it imposter syndrome? Is she doing
enough? She compares herself to peers on LinkedIn and Instagram. She knows therapy exists but
can't afford it consistently and struggles to justify it when she's "not bad enough."

### Pain Points
- Anxiety spikes on Sunday evenings (Sunday scaries)
- Can't switch off from work — checks Slack at 10pm
- Feels like she should have it more figured out by now
- Worried about being judged if she admits she's struggling

### What She Needs
- Quick emotional check-ins she can do in 5 minutes
- Someone to validate that her feelings make sense
- Tools that don't feel like homework
- No judgment about not being "sick enough" to get help

### Content angles for Ava
- "POV: anxiety brain at 2am"
- "Nobody tells you about the pressure of your first real job"
- "What if I told you 5 minutes could change your Sunday nights"

### Visual direction
Mid-twenties, South Asian or East Asian woman, natural hair, home desk or sofa, warm early
evening light. Slightly tired but finding calm.

## Persona 2 — Mindful Marcus

**Age:** 31
**Location:** Suburban / mid-size city (Austin, Manchester, Toronto)
**Occupation:** Software engineer / tech worker

### Background
Marcus is good at his job and has reasonable financial stability, but he's been feeling
disconnected — from his partner, from himself, from meaning. He's tried meditation apps but they
feel passive. He doesn't identify with "mental health struggles" in a clinical sense but knows
something is off. He's curious and analytical; he wants to understand his patterns.

### Pain Points
- Burnout from always being "on" — remote work blurred all boundaries
- Hard to articulate what's wrong (nothing is dramatically bad)
- Finds most mental health content too soft or too clinical
- Doesn't want to be told to "just meditate more"

### What He Needs
- Insight, not just soothing — he wants to understand his patterns
- Practical tools with a clear "why"
- Something that respects his intelligence
- Low-friction daily habit, not a big commitment

### Content angles for Marcus
- "What burnout actually looks like when you're still functional"
- "This is what happens when you don't process stress"
- "5 minutes of reflection that actually tells you something"

### Visual direction
Early thirties, Black man, casual clothes (t-shirt, joggers), home office or kitchen, morning
light. Thoughtful expression, not in distress — more "figuring something out."

## Persona 3 — Curious Carla

**Age:** 42
**Location:** Suburban (Chicago, Melbourne, Dublin)
**Occupation:** Operations manager / parent

### Background
Carla has a full life: two kids, a demanding job, a partner who means well but doesn't always
get it. She's been running on empty for years and has normalised it. She would never describe
herself as struggling with mental health — that's for other people. She recently started
noticing that she's snapping more, sleeping less, and finding nothing enjoyable. A friend
mentioned Aurie and she downloaded it out of curiosity.

### Pain Points
- No time for anything — every hour is already spoken for
- Feels guilty prioritising herself
- Doesn't recognise herself as someone who needs support
- Dismisses her own feelings as "just normal parenting"

### What She Needs
- Low-time-commitment support (she doesn't have an hour for therapy)
- Validation that burnout is real and she's not just weak
- Something she can use in the car, at lunch, between meetings
- No shame around starting

### Content angles for Carla
- "Parent burnout is real — and it's not just being tired"
- "Nobody talks about how depleted mums (and dads) actually are"
- "3 minutes in the car park before school pickup changed my day"

### Visual direction
Early forties, Latina or white woman, lived-in home setting (kitchen, hallway), after-school
light. Parent-tired but warm — a small exhale of relief.
